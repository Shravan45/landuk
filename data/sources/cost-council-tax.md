# Council Tax bands

Council Tax is a local tax charged by every council in England, Scotland, and Wales to fund local services (bins, policing, libraries, roads). Northern Ireland uses a separate domestic rates system instead.

## How bands work

In England and Scotland, every home is placed into one of 8 bands (A to H) based on what its value would have been on 1 April 1991 (regardless of what it's actually worth today). Wales has 9 bands (A to I), revalued in 2003.

| Band | England: value range (1991 prices) |
|------|-------------------------------------|
| A | Up to £40,000 |
| B | £40,001 – £52,000 |
| C | £52,001 – £68,000 |
| D | £68,001 – £88,000 |
| E | £88,001 – £120,000 |
| F | £120,001 – £160,000 |
| G | £160,001 – £320,000 |
| H | More than £320,000 |

Band D is used as the standard reference figure for comparing council tax levels between different local authorities, since bills for other bands are calculated as a fixed proportion of the Band D rate (e.g. Band A is 6/9ths of Band D, Band H is 18/9ths of Band D).

## What affects your bill

- Your property's band and your local council's set rates (these vary significantly between councils — a Band D bill can be roughly twice as expensive in the highest-charging council areas as the lowest).
- A single-person household gets a 25% discount.
- Full-time students are exempt.
- Some properties and people qualify for additional discounts, exemptions, or Council Tax Reduction (means-tested support), especially for low-income households.

## Checking your band

You can look up any property's Council Tax band for free on GOV.UK before renting or buying, so you can budget accurately. Newly built homes are assessed and assigned a band when first occupied.
