# UK cost of living overview for new arrivals

These are indicative, order-of-magnitude figures compiled from ONS private rental market statistics and general UK household spending patterns, intended to help someone relocating budget roughly by region. They are not live figures — always check the Office for National Statistics (ONS) Private Rent and House Prices bulletin and current listings for up-to-date numbers before committing to a lease.

## Typical monthly rent (1-bedroom flat, indicative ranges)

- **Inner London**: roughly £1,700–£2,400
- **Outer London / commuter towns near London**: roughly £1,100–£1,600
- **Other major cities (Manchester, Birmingham, Edinburgh, Bristol, Leeds)**: roughly £750–£1,150
- **Mid-sized cities and towns**: roughly £550–£850
- **Rural areas / smaller towns**: roughly £450–£700

Rent for a 2-bedroom or family-sized home is typically 30–60% higher than the equivalent 1-bedroom figure in the same area, and shared housing (a room in a house-share) is typically 40–60% cheaper than a self-contained 1-bedroom flat.

## Other recurring costs (indicative, per adult per month unless noted)

- **Council Tax**: roughly £100–£220/month for a typical Band C/D home, split between however many liable adults live there (see the Council Tax bands source for how banding works).
- **Utilities (gas, electricity, water) for a typical household**: roughly £150–£280/month, higher in winter and for larger properties.
- **Broadband**: roughly £25–£40/month.
- **Mobile phone plan**: roughly £10–£30/month depending on data allowance.
- **Public transport**: a monthly travelcard in a major city typically runs £100–£220; in London specifically, Zones 1–2 travel is around £160–£180/month, rising with more zones.
- **Groceries**: roughly £200–£350/month for one person depending on diet and shopping habits.
- **TV Licence** (required if watching or streaming live TV, or using BBC iPlayer): a flat annual fee, payable regardless of location.

## Regional cost-of-living index (rough comparison, London = 100)

- London: 100 (baseline, most expensive)
- South East England: ~80–90
- Scotland (Edinburgh/Glasgow): ~65–75
- Other major English cities (Manchester, Birmingham, Leeds, Bristol): ~60–75
- Wales, Northern England, Northern Ireland (outside major cities): ~55–65

## Budgeting tip

A commonly used rule of thumb is that rent should not exceed roughly 30–35% of net (after-tax) income, though this is difficult to stick to in London and the South East, where many renters spend 40%+ of income on housing.
